Case Study 1: BTK Killer – The Role of Digital Forensics

Location: Wichita, Kansas, USA | Year: 2005

1. Introduction

The BTK Killer was an American serial killer who used the name BTK, meaning "Bind, Torture, Kill". He committed murders in Wichita, Kansas, and sent letters to the police and media. Digital forensic evidence played an important role in identifying him.

2. Background of the Case

Between 1974 and 1991, ten people were murdered in Wichita, Kansas. The killer remained unidentified for many years. In 2004, he resumed communication with the police and media, giving investigators an opportunity to examine the information he sent.

3. Incident Details

In February 2005, the killer sent a floppy disk to a television station. Investigators examined the disk and recovered a deleted Microsoft Word document. The document contained metadata that provided important clues about the person who had used it.

4. Digital Evidence

  • Floppy Disk: The storage device sent by the killer.
  • Deleted Word Document: Investigators recovered a deleted document from the disk.
  • Last Saved By: The document showed the name "Dennis".
  • Organization: The metadata referred to "Christ Lutheran Church".
  • Document Metadata: Information stored in the document helped investigators trace its previous use.

5. Investigation

  1. Examination of the Floppy Disk: Investigators examined the disk for stored and deleted data.
  2. Recovery of Deleted Data: A deleted Word document was recovered from the disk.
  3. Metadata Analysis: The document properties showed the name "Dennis" and the organization "Christ Lutheran Church".
  4. Identification of the Suspect: Investigators searched the clues and identified Dennis Rader, who was associated with the church.
  5. DNA Examination: DNA evidence obtained from a medical sample belonging to Rader's daughter provided a familial match with DNA from the crime scenes.
  6. Connecting the Evidence: Digital clues and DNA evidence helped investigators build the case against Rader.

6. Findings

  • A deleted Word document was recovered from the floppy disk.
  • The document metadata contained the name "Dennis".
  • The metadata also referred to Christ Lutheran Church.
  • These clues helped investigators identify Dennis Rader as a suspect.
  • DNA evidence provided additional support for the investigation.

7. Outcome / Result

Dennis Rader was arrested in February 2005. He admitted to murdering ten people and later pleaded guilty. He was sentenced to ten consecutive life sentences.

8. Conclusion

This case shows how deleted files and document metadata can provide important clues during a digital forensic investigation. The information recovered from the floppy disk helped investigators identify a suspect, while other evidence supported the case.

Exam Tip: For a 20-mark answer, give special attention to Digital Evidence, Investigation and Findings.

Case Study 2: Silk Road – Investigation of Ross Ulbricht

Location: United States | Year: 2013

1. Introduction

Silk Road was an illegal online marketplace that operated on the Dark Web. It was used to buy and sell drugs and other unlawful goods. Ross Ulbricht operated the website using the online identity "Dread Pirate Roberts" (DPR). Digital forensics helped investigators identify him and collect evidence.

2. Background of the Case

Silk Road was created in January 2011. It used the Tor network to hide the identities and locations of its users. Payments were mainly made using Bitcoin. The marketplace operated until October 2013, when law-enforcement authorities shut it down.

3. Incident Details

Silk Road allowed users to trade illegal products through an anonymous online platform. Thousands of buyers and sellers used the website. Investigators began examining the website, its operator and the digital activity connected with the identity "Dread Pirate Roberts".

4. Digital Evidence

  • Seized Laptop: A laptop recovered from Ross Ulbricht containing important digital records.
  • Website Records: Data connected with the operation of the Silk Road marketplace.
  • Chat Messages: Digital communications related to the website and its activities.
  • Server Data: Information recovered from the website's server.
  • Bitcoin Transactions: Financial records used to examine payments connected with the marketplace.
  • Online Identity and Posts: Online activity that helped investigators connect the suspect with the identity "Dread Pirate Roberts".

5. Investigation

  1. Online Investigation: Investigators studied Silk Road and its online activities.
  2. Tracing the Suspect: They examined online information and clues connected with the website's operator.
  3. Undercover Activity: Investigators used undercover accounts and made purchases to understand the marketplace.
  4. Server Examination: Digital data from the seized server was examined for evidence about the website and its operation.
  5. Laptop Examination: The laptop seized from Ross Ulbricht was examined for records, messages and other relevant data.
  6. Bitcoin Investigation: Financial transactions were studied to trace payments connected with Silk Road.
  7. Evidence Collection: Investigators combined the digital and financial evidence to support the case against Ulbricht.

6. Findings

  • Ross Ulbricht was identified as the creator and operator of Silk Road.
  • He used the online identity "Dread Pirate Roberts".
  • The website was used for illegal online trade.
  • Bitcoin was used to make payments on the marketplace.
  • Records from the laptop and server provided evidence connected with the website's operation.

7. Outcome / Result

Ross Ulbricht was arrested on 1 October 2013 in San Francisco. Authorities seized his laptop and shut down Silk Road. In February 2015, he was found guilty on all seven charges. In May 2015, he was sentenced to life imprisonment.

8. Conclusion

The Silk Road case shows how digital forensic investigation can use computer devices, server records, online activity and financial information to investigate cybercrime. The examination of digital evidence helped authorities identify the person operating the marketplace and build a case against him.
